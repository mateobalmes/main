Recruiter call preparation

Product Manager, Martech — Marketing Activation

Air CanadaToronto, on-siteCampaign Classic + Journey Optimizer
They are asking for four years of Campaign Classic, which is their present. You have three years of Journey Optimizer and Experience Platform at Tim Hortons scale, which is their destination. Do not defend the gap. Reframe the role around where the stack is going, and be the only candidate on the list who has already built there.

The angle

Read this first. Everything else follows from it.

The posting has a tell in it. They want a v7 to v8 upgrade led, and separately they want Journey Optimizer owned, Experience Platform audiences activated into both tools, and offer decisioning stood up. That is a shop with a legacy email platform and an ambition to run real-time orchestration on top of a CDP. The Campaign Classic requirement describes the work in front of them. The AJO and AEP requirements describe where they are trying to get.

Most candidates who clear the four-year Campaign Classic bar will be deep in the legacy half and thin on the new half. You are the reverse, and the reverse is rarer. At Tim Hortons you ran the destination stack in production across 4,000 locations for three years, including the exact integration surface they name in the posting: Experience Platform schemas aligned to activation, dataflows from the CDP, and consent enforced before send.

So the line you hold all call is this. The Campaign Classic operating knowledge is learnable in a quarter, and you have a plan for it. The Experience Platform and Journey Optimizer judgment they need is the part that takes years, and you already have it.

One thing to decide before you dial: the CV you sent leads with a summary that says Campaign Classic is ecosystem exposure only. That was the right call and it buys you enormous credibility, because it means everything else you say is trusted. Do not walk it back on the phone. Own it in one sentence and move to the bridge.

Answer out loud, then reveal. Mark what came out badly and replay only those. Questions 4 and 5 decide the call.

Two reasons. First, the shape of it. Most postings split the roadmap from the build, and this one deliberately doesn't, which is how I've always worked best. Second, the direction. You're running Campaign and standing up Journey Optimizer with Experience Platform audiences feeding both, and that transition is the exact work I've just spent three years doing on the other side of it.

The posting

Summarized section by section from the listing you sent, so you can scan it on the call. Keep the original open in another tab if you want their exact wording.

Product Manager, Marketing Technology, sitting on the Marketing Technology team and covering Marketing Activation. The job is to define and deliver platform strategy for Adobe Campaign Classic and Adobe Journey Optimizer: roadmap, prioritization, platform standards, and hands-on configuration of the activation layer across workflows, journeys, integrations and templates.

They state the boundary twice. You build the platform capabilities that Marketing Operations uses to run email and triggered journeys. Marketing Technology owns the platforms; Marketing Operations executes campaigns on them. You are accountable for product direction and hands-on platform work, not for running day-to-day campaigns. Their summary of the mandate is to make activation reliable, compliant and adoptable.

The actual work

What the posting tells you they will put in front of you, in the interviews and in the job.

On this call

A recruiter screen does not test platform depth properly. Expect verification rather than evaluation: confirm the Campaign Classic years, confirm the AJO years, confirm on-site, confirm French, confirm comp and notice. Your job on this call is to survive the Campaign Classic filter and get to the hiring manager, which means giving your bridge answer early and clearly enough that the recruiter can repeat it to someone else without garbling it. Write that sentence for them.

Likely at the hiring manager round

  • Whiteboard the dataflow from the CDP into both Campaign and AJO, with the data contract and latency expectations named. Their cross-platform section is written as an architecture responsibility. This is your strongest possible exercise, so hope for it.
  • Debug a scenario where the audience count in the CDP does not match what was sent. The SQL requirement exists for this. Answer with the order you check layers in.
  • Describe how you would take a v7 to v8 migration through their phases, especially cutover and hypercare. Be honest about not having done this specific upgrade, then show the method.
  • Explain the standard you would set for batch email in Campaign versus triggered messaging in AJO. A direct responsibility bullet, and a genuine judgment question rather than a knowledge one.
  • How you would raise Marketing Operations self-serve maturity from where it is today. Your runbooks and training work at Tim Hortons answers this directly.

Possible take-home or panel exercise

Roles written this way often ask for a short ninety-day plan or a platform assessment memo. If they do, structure it as assessment, then the three things you would stabilize first, then the roadmap. Do not present a roadmap without an assessment in front of it. If they ask for a business case, use the format you already know from presenting to Director and VP level at Restaurant Brands.

The job itself, year one

Reading the posting as a work plan rather than a wish list, year one is probably three things running in parallel. Carry the Campaign upgrade through cutover without breaking sends. Stand up or mature Journey Optimizer for real-time and lifecycle triggers, including offer decisioning. And make the CDP the single source of audiences and consent for both tools, which is the part that fails quietly and costs the most when it does.

Underneath all three sits the same recurring task: documentation, standards and enablement so Marketing Operations can run without you. They mention key-person risk explicitly, which usually means someone recently left and took the platform knowledge with them.

What success looks like

They published their own KPI list, which is unusual and useful. Say it back to them with a view on which to fix first.

Their five measures

  • Time to launch. How long from campaign request to send. Falls when templates and workflow patterns are reusable, rises when every campaign is bespoke.
  • Workflow reuse. The share of sends built from standard patterns rather than from scratch. The clearest single proxy for whether the platform work is landing.
  • Deliverability health. Reputation, bounce and complaint rates, and whether warm-up and suppression are holding.
  • Journey throughput. Volume and reliability of triggered and real-time journeys in AJO, which only grows once the event and audience plumbing is trustworthy.
  • Marketing Operations self-serve maturity. How much they can do without you. The one that determines whether the role is strategic or a service desk.

What good looks like at each mark

Ninety days. You know the platform inside out, including what is undocumented. The migration plan is assessed and sequenced with the cutover risks named. You have met Marketing Operations and know which three things frustrate them most. You have not changed much yet, deliberately.

Six months. The upgrade is through cutover and in hypercare with no material send incidents. Workflow templates and targeting standards exist and are being used. Audience and consent enforcement from the CDP is verified rather than assumed in both tools.

Twelve months. Time to launch has measurably dropped, reuse is the default, journeys run at volume with offer decisioning live, and Marketing Operations builds most of what it needs without escalating. The platform is documented well enough that your absence would not stop it.

If they ask which KPI you would fix first, do not answer with a metric. Answer with the order: reliability and consent enforcement first because everything downstream depends on it, then reuse and time to launch, then throughput and self-serve. Sequence reads as judgment; a favourite metric reads as a guess.

What to ask

Pick three, close on the last.

  • Where is the Journey Optimizer build in its lifecycle relative to the Campaign v7 to v8 upgrade, and which one is the priority in year one? The single most useful question you can ask. If AJO is the priority, your profile is the strongest on the list and the Campaign gap shrinks. If the upgrade is the priority, you learn that early.
  • The posting draws a firm line between Marketing Technology owning the platforms and Marketing Operations running campaigns. How settled is that split today? Unusual clarity in a posting often means it was recently contested.
  • How far along is the Experience Platform work, and are audiences already activating into Campaign and AJO or is that still to build?
  • Who does the role report to, and how large is the Marketing Technology team?
  • What are the next steps and the timeline? Always close on this.
